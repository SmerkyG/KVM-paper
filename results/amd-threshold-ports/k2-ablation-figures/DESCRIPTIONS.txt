K2 Horizon hyperparameter ablations at 64K

The studies use K2 Horizon with FP8 weights and BF16 KV on MI325X, at
batch one. The full RULER sweeps match the same requests across settings.
The dotted line in the RULER panels is archived full attention quality on
the same requests. The latency measurements use separate batch-one
serving runs.

01  Region mass threshold

We varied the region mass cutoff in prefill and decode separately, keeping
the other phase at the standard threshold. The cutoff is the mean region
mass multiplied by c. Lower cutoffs let more regions
open, and scores stayed close to the standard setting. Tightening the
cutoff too far hurt both phases, with a pronounced Multikey-2 drop in
prefill. The middle setting gave the best overall quality on this panel.

02  Fixed region budgets

We tried opening the best four, eight, or sixteen eligible regions per
query head, using the same normalized region scores as threshold routing.
Changing one phase at a time kept the comparison focused on the budget rule.
The smallest budget saved a little prefill time but caused the clearest
quality loss. Larger budgets recovered some quality. The widest decode
budget came closest to threshold quality. All three tested fixed selectors
took longer in decode than their paired threshold controls.

03  Centroid growth schedule

We halved and doubled the standard multiplier in the square-root centroid
schedule, holding threshold routing and the decode window fixed. Fewer
centroids sped up prefill but reduced quality. More centroids modestly
improved quality over the standard LoD setting while increasing prefill
time. The small score difference versus dense was within the paired
uncertainty and came mainly from two requests. Decode time changed
much less in the preliminary serving timings.

04  Decode window size

We varied the maximum length of the exact local window while holding the
BSWA block size and cache catch-up interval fixed. A wider window keeps
more preceding blocks exact throughout the current block. The smaller and
larger windows both scored close to the standard window, and the
preliminary decode timings were nearly the same.

05  BSWA block size

We tested shorter and longer decode blocks, sweeping from no complete
preceding blocks through three. The cache catch-up interval followed the
block size. Each series used its own fresh control at the standard block
and window. Keeping no preceding block gave the weakest quality in each
series. Keeping at least one brought the scores close to the controls.
The paired serving measurements showed little change in decode speed.

06  Prefill block length

We changed the first exact block, scheduler chunk, and prefill cache
catch-up together, with the later exact lookback held fixed. We measured
prompt loss on paired ProLong documents and latency through the first
decode token to include deferred cache work. The dashed line marks the
16K LoD control. The dotted line shows dense full attention loss on
the same documents and is close to the 16K loss. The 1K block used
substantially less reserved memory than 16K, with higher latency and
nearly unchanged prompt loss. The original 512 result used stale
cache entries. After the handoff repair, its loss stayed close to the
16K control. It saved a little more memory than 1K and took longer.
The 256-token block saved a little more memory again, with slightly
higher loss and more than twice the 16K latency. The 2K block gave a smaller memory saving and a smaller latency
penalty than 1K. The 4K and 8K blocks saved little memory. The
32K block had similar latency to 16K and used substantially more
reserved memory.

07  Three-tier page refinement

We compared two-tier region opening with one, two, or four pages per
opened region and an adaptive page cutoff. The arms shared the same
prefill policy, region threshold, and prompts across all RULER tasks.
One page caused the largest quality loss, concentrated in Multikey-2
and Multikey-3. Two pages recovered much of the loss. Four pages and
the adaptive cutoff matched the two-tier scores on both Multikey tasks
and stayed close to it across the full panel. In the Multikey diagnostic,
four pages used far fewer exact entries than adaptive refinement. In
the separate serving timings, four pages took longer to decode than
adaptive refinement. Two-tier had the lowest measured decode latency.

08  Region mass threshold Pareto frontier

Each frontier shows the best measured quality as the latency budget
increases. We varied the threshold in one phase at a time and measured
absolute latency on the same ProLong prompt used to time dense full
attention. The standard threshold scored close to dense on the matched
RULER cohort. Looser prefill cutoffs took substantially longer with
little change in quality. The loosest prefill setting needed a smaller
internal query tile to fit in memory. Tightening the prefill cutoff saved time at
the cost of RULER accuracy. Tightening the decode cutoff changed
latency much less.

09  BSWA block size Pareto frontier

The chart plots absolute decode latency for each local-window recipe
and for dense full attention. The standard LoD points summarize the
paired 256-token control timings. Keeping at least one preceding block
recovered most of the quality lost when only the current block was
exact. Differences within the same-GPU LoD pairs stayed below one
percent, and dense decode took longer than the LoD settings on this
prompt.
